## What it does

<!-- Stable description of the program. Carried forward from the previous note; edit only when the product itself changes.
     3 to 6 lines: what it is, who uses it, where it runs (URL/port/binary), how to start it. -->
65,536 articulated bodies, each with 74 degrees of freedom, contacts and 50 actuators, step together in 66.7 ms
on one AMD RX 7900 XTX. Kinestride is batched GPU physics in Mojo: one GPU thread per body, any MuJoCo MJCF model
as input, and every stage checked against MuJoCo 3.9.0 with a planted bug that the check must catch.

## Highlights

<!-- 1 to 5 bullets a user would notice. Lead with the benefit, not the implementation. -->
- Watch a brain live: `brain_view.mojo` draws every neuron of a simulated brain (FlyWire: 138,639) as a point lit by its
  spikes, in 96 us per frame; fly-connectome's engine window shows it as the Brain panel with what the DNs trigger.
- The engine has a real window: run controls, per-fly cards and the brain view in imkit panels drawn on the GPU,
  replacing mpv and the Tk window (fly-connectome `WATCH_VIEW=gui`).

## Known limits

<!-- Honest list: slow paths, unsupported cases, flaky upstreams, anything UNVERIFIED. -->
- The brain view is points only (no neurites), orthographic, no picking; two lit neurons are hard to see among 138k
  resting ones (the Brain panel's trigger strip names them).
- The brain worker's tap costs ~0.5 ms per write (target 50 us); the GUI taps every 5th control step.
- Checked on one GPU (RX 7900 XTX) and one desktop (KDE Wayland).
